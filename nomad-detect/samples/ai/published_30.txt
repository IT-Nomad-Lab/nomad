Every data platform I have run ended up with a second system bolted on whose only job was to notice what changed in the first one. We paid to store the data, paid again to watch it, and paid a third time when the two disagreed.

That third payment was always the worst. Tens of thousands lost in production data. Early morning calls about PII that had to be handled with care in a format that fought every delete. Long hours explaining why a report disagreed with its own source.

And deletes had their own tax. Removing a few rows meant rewriting enormous files, so some of my most expensive compute went to making data go away.

Iceberg v3 just went into Public Preview on Databricks, and the three features that matter are aimed at exactly those taxes. Row lineage: every row carries its own ID and change sequence, so the table notices its own changes. The bolt-on dies. Deletion vectors: a delete writes a small file instead of rewriting a huge one. VARIANT: semi-structured data gets a real column type instead of a string dump and a prayer.

None of this will headline a keynote. The keynotes belong to v4, which you still cannot touch.

Nobody puts a deletion vector on a slide. That is usually the sign it matters.
