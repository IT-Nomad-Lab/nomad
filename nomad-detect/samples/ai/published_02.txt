61% of enterprise AI projects were approved on projected ROI that nobody ever measured after launch. But this is not an AI problem. It is an IT industry problem.

That number comes from a recent analysis tracking thousands of enterprise AI deployments. It is not an anomaly.

Every January, companies publish roadmaps, approve budgets, and commit to outcomes. By March, and sometimes even sooner, the market has shifted. By June the team is chasing a new priority. The original promises are quietly shelved. Nobody calls it failure. Nobody calls it anything.

I've seen this specifically in R&D. A team spends months building an AI feature. Before it is validated, a new trend appears. The feature gets abandoned. The cycle repeats. The business case from January is never revisited.

Technology moves fast. Markets move faster. That is real. But speed is not the reason nobody checks the numbers. The reason is governance. Or the absence of it.

Governance is not bureaucracy. It is the mechanism that forces you to answer one simple question: did we deliver what we said we would?

Without it, every broken promise becomes a strategy pivot.

How does your company hold itself accountable to the plans it approves at the start of the year?
