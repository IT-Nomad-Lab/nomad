**The Silent Failure of Dashboards: Why They Often Miss the Mark**

As a seasoned business leader, I've had the privilege of working with numerous organizations that have invested heavily in dashboards to drive data-driven decision making. While dashboards can be incredibly powerful tools, I've come to realize that they often fall short of their intended purpose. In my experience, many dashboards fail to change decisions because they:

* Focus too much on metrics and not enough on context and narrative
* Rely on historical data rather than providing forward-looking insights
* Are often siloed, failing to connect the dots between different functions and teams
* Lack a clear and compelling story to drive action

I've seen it time and time again: a beautiful, high-tech dashboard is launched with great fanfare, only to be met with eye-rolling and skepticism from leaders who fail to see the value in the data presented. Why? Because the data is presented in a way that's too dry, too technical, or too focused on the wrong metrics.

To truly drive change, dashboards need to be designed with the decision-maker in mind. They need to tell a story that's relevant, timely, and actionable. They need to connect the dots between different functions and teams, providing a clear and compelling picture of the organization's performance.

So, let's not just build dashboards for the sake of building dashboards. Let's build dashboards that drive real change.
